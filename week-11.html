Finalising workshop details

I reached out to potential participants, although we haven't settled on a date yet, as most of my lovely participants are really busy. Most likely it'll be held in Nov-Dec of 2026. Let's get the workshops out of the way before the year ends!

I also discussed the details of the workshop activity with Shu Min. It will be a round robin exercise, which is focused around building the world/universe of this speculative object, something I was very worried about. I need the imaginations of other people!

Progress on the speculative objects

I really need to come up with a name soon. Typing "Speculative Objects" exhausts me and is quite vague. Anyways, I came up with some ideas for the speculative timekeeping devices, some of which were iterations of previous ideas. I didn't manage to come up with as many ideas as I had hoped—I was hoping to come up with 50 ideas which I now realise is a fool's dream. In reality, I came up with 3 #FLOP.

The feedback that I received was that I should start thinking of how I was going to produce these sketches. I was initially thinking of 3D modelling it, but this could take quite a long time as I have not touched Blender in many moons. Shu Min advised me to produce in such a way that there was still room for me to explore, rather than being overly fixated on finalising the form of the "clocks". After much thinking, I realised that I could do some photo manipulation on photoshop. Ashlee also very generously offered to teach me 3D modelling (thanks Ashlee!!).

Reflection of the week
I would like to open this week's reflection with a celebration! I have found my way out of the maze. I think I have a better idea of what I want to do. I'm a little embarrassed to say that the "new" plan is actually the old plan, I just needed more confidence to execute it. I did not do much this week to be honest, but I'm glad that I've finally found my footing. The map is in my hand, and I'm finding my way out of the forest! I'm suffering, but I'm happy about it. (╥ ω ╥) The survey responses made me feel much more sure of the direction and secure about the realistic-ness of the project. It also made me feel a little sad to look at everyone's answers.
