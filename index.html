01

Visual Complexity: Mapping Patterns of Information

Manuel Lima

This book presents one hundred interesting examples of information visualisation on the planet, analysing how "our ability to generate information now far exceeds our capacity to understand it". I found that this was a fitting book as it talks about how complex data are converted to easily digestible visual diagrams complete with signs and symbols that we somehow understand.

02

Slow Technology Reader: A Tool for Shaping Divergent Futures

Carolyn F. Strauss (ed.)

This book gathers contributions from various fields to consider technology in a "slow" lens. Chapters like "Restoration, Rest and Respite" and "Fast and Slow and In-Between" helped me in the initial brainstorming phase especially in linking the topic of time-keeping to a design context.

03

Einstein's Dreams

Alan Lightman

A whimsical, fictional account of how Einstein dreamt up his theories of time and retold it to his friend Besso. The most intriguing theories were how time = distance (which is partially true??) so when a girl runs out of her home into the distance, she instantly ages, which is a terrifying thought. Another chapter talks about how people set up homes in the mountain as it was proven that time moves slower at a greater height (the opposite is true), to age more slowly.

Media

01

Arrival

Dir. by Denis Villeneuve

Adapted from the 1998 novella "Stories of your life" by Ted Chiang, Arrival follows linguist Louise Banks who is tasked to decipher the language of the mysterious Heptapods (the aliens) who landed on Earth. I really liked this movie as it really got me thinking about how time flowed differently for different lifeforms. Its so interesting how they also have a vastly different language system(s)that reflects the way they think about time.

02

The Good Place S3E4
"Jeremy Bearimy"

Dir. by Michael Schur

A really funny TV show that explores the idea of the afterlife, with heaven being known as the "good place" (hence the name). In this particular episode, Michael (Ted Danson) explains that the way time passes in the afterlife is vastly different from Earth. He visualises this through a complicated circular yet linear diagram that looks like the name "Jeremy Bearimy" which is why time is known as "Jeremy Bearimy".

03

Avengers: Endgame

Dir. by Anthony and Joe Russo

The concluding movie to the Avengers series. I remember being very intrigued by the time heist plot as it saw time travel in a completely different direction.
